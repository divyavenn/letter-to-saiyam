Dear Saiyam,

I am on the plane to San Salvador, working and listening to a kind of [husky, soaring Indian song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-rV6fTSSaI), full of pure yearning. Maybe it’s silly to yearn for you when I’ve been apart from you for less than a day, but it seems I do. Do you remember when I said I wasn’t a yearner and you said “Well, I am”?  It stopped me short in a beautiful and terrible way. How the tables turn…

It’s not that time away from you is inherently terrible. I often have a lot of fun, even just being alone in my room puttering around or working with full concentration. But if there’s any empty space in my mind, it echoes with you. 

I loved watching Ford vs Ferrari with you yesterday. I really want to undertake something with you, a renovation or a robot or learning rumba. I want to sleep with you even when I'm exhausted from it, I want to lie in bed with you even when I’m itching to get up and do things, and eat with you when I’m not actually hungry. I think because it’s closeness I want, and all standard forms of intimacy are hedonistic ones. But there’s intimacy in shared discipline, shared toil, and shared learning too. 

Whatever compels me so strongly and irrationally towards you, I hope it lasts always.

I think you’d really like some of the indian songs I listen to. For whatever reason I’m embarrassed about playing them, it feels a little cringe for no good reason. But they make me think of spinning slowly in dark rooms, of pigeons scattered by a gust of wind, of summer storms, of your face in candlelight. 

I should stop saying things over and over, even if it’s the impulsive expression of a genuine sentiment. How often can I say things like “I love these daises” and “I love my backpack” and “I love the weather in SF” before I sound like a blank old woman, all smiles and talcum and dementia? 

I really do need to learn to build good agent harnesses. I want to try building a local agent on my computer, one I know inside and out. I also want to dip my toes into cybersecurity, I know pitifully little about it. I didn’t bring my sketchbook (or my keyboard) because I believe in packing light, but I miss it already….You would really like drawing, I think. There’s nothing more meditative. I am going to download a book on control theory. 

Sincerely,
Divya
