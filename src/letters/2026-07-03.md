Dear Saiyam, 

The river was perfect. Utterly clear and sweet-smelling and soothing to touch. As we set off in the raft, we passed a man and woman having a private romantic swim. He waved and grinned while she hid behind him, as far in the water as she could sink, and clung shyly to his hips. I don’t normally find timidity endearing, but it was lovely. 

The gorge was filled with the most luxurious, sprawling greenery. We saw cormorants and kingfishers and a spider so large Abhi yelped and sprang to his feet recklessly in center of the raft. There were so many stretches of white sand along the riverbank, draped with vines and willows. I kept thinking how much you and I would have enjoyed a picnic and a swim on those private shores.

![river-2](/images/river-2.png)

We actually did have a picnic of sorts. The instructor passed around parcels of food wrapped in banana leaves: rice, grilled plantain, some sort of fried patty (made from amaranth for me, meat for them), half a boiled egg, half an avocado, and cups of aguapanela (sugarcane juice with lime). Aguapanela is the greatest drink in the world; “nectar of the Gods” as Dhruv put it.  What a shame they don’t have it in the states! What a shame you can’t buy hearty, balanced lunches packed in banana leaves! We sat on the river rocks and breakfasted like kings.

![river-lunch](/images/river-lunch.png)

It was a two hour car ride to the Rio San Michel and back. The car ride may have been ever better than the river - the glorious Columbian countryside, the shitty roads, the chickens and cows and children all running free, all sorts of aimless chatter. One day I really want to get a few friends together, hire a driver, and roadtrip around Latin America.

![river-resto](/images/river-resto.png)

Things went downhill later, I’m afraid, as my startup didn’t want to extend the contract period and it felt much like a rejection. I was low energy already from lack of sleep, and this only made it worse. I felt like Dorothy Parker: **This living, this living, this living/ Was never a project of mine.**

Abhi started to make me feel better by helping me analyze everything I could have done better while Dhruv glared daggers at him for being so callous. I hastened to clarify I preferred actionable advice to any form of reassurance. I think I’ve become such good friends with these people because I share a flaw with each of them: chaos-seeking, undisciplined yapping with Shaya, insensitive and autistic levels of truth-seeking and first principles-reasoning with Abhi, and moodiness with Dhruv. Anyway, their insights into my shortcomings are razor-sharp and very actionable; I think that may be one of the things I maximally value in friends. What a strange world this is, the way human connection waxes and wanes. So many times I’ve thought I’d be close to a set of people forever, only to be entirely out of touch a year later. It seems almost ridiculous to let strangers, once again, feel like family in a matter of months. Now that I am where I have always wanted to be, ready to tunnel roots into misty hills, maybe it will last.

![river-selfie](/images/river-selfie.png)

I tried to approach a bunch of Columbian girls at the club to drink with us. The girls seemed happy and excited to talk to me until I beckoned Abhi and Dhruv over. “We’ll catch you later,” one of them said coldly, and she and her friends shot us nasty looks the rest of the night whenever we glanced in their direction. They may have concluded I was an odd sort of female pimp.


In these seven days of adventure I understand very much what Penelope meant to Odysseus. Merely the idea of her, the longing for refuge in moments of frustration or tiredness or irritation. I hope you don’t find this mildly insulting; I think we take turns. I have been your Penelope before, waiting for you through long dreary days as you live life, in your own spectacular, to the fullest.

As Robert Fitzgerald translated Odysseus’s homecoming:

His dear wife, clear and faithful, in his arms,
Longed for as the sun warmed earth is longed for by a swimmer
Few men can keep alive through a big surf to crawl, clotted with brine, on kindly beaches
And so she too rejoiced, her gaze upon her husband, her white arms round him pressed as though forever.
