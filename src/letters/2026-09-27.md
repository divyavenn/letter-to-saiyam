Dear Saiyam, 

On Thursday Shaya told me that he’d get the ick if a girl wasn’t warm and maternal, that one thing he really didn’t like about Avni was her coldness/tough-love attitude. With a sinking heart I saw again, through your eyes, my generally dismissive attitude towards children and all the outrageous things I’ve said. I don’t know why I emphasize these ugly feelings so much. Maybe as some sort of rebellion against the lifelong assumption that I, as a girl, ought to be enamored with children. Maybe I’m projecting my contempt for women - the way they coddle and spoil themselves and any creature under their care, often to the long-term detriment of that creature. Maybe I prefer you be pleasantly surprised by emergent maternal instincts than be shocked seeing me implacable, making a child crying with fear go and do the very thing that terrifies her. I don’t despise weak people, like I say, truly. I despise people who don’t try to be strong. The harder they try, the more I love them. Perhaps the weaker you are the more you have to try, and I’m destined to adore children - some children - after all. 

I remember the exact moment I loved Ranjani. I didn’t like her much for the first six years of our acquaintance. She cried a lot and followed me around like a fawn, and lacked, so I thought, any defiance or imagination.

I was around 7 or 8, maybe 9. My uncle took us and her friends sledding. The day suddenly went cruelly cold, so cold our faces were in very real pain and we could barely walk in the wind and snow. The other little girls sat on the sleds and wailed, being pulled along by their fathers. I was trudging, my hands in my pockets, feeling grumpy, only to see Ranjani manfully battling the wind, not only walking on her own but pulling a sled behind her. I was filled with admiration for her, and shame at my own selfishness, so I went and pulled a sled too. 

I saw a tweet that made me anticipate the emotional experience of parenthood differently. Some new parent articulated: “it feels more like loving your partner than you’d think.” 

It reminded me of a book called Hold Me Tight, which says that attachment theory, talked about so much between parent and child, applies equally intensely to love. That in a deep romantic bond, any threat of loss feels existential. During conflict, that fear must be pacified before reason can take over.

Today my aunt said that when someone speaks well or ill of your children, you feel it even more than they do. I thought of the tweet, and of the book, and of how them complimenting you hit harder than any compliment I’ve ever gotten. I was ravenous for that praise, I fished for it subtly but shamelessly in every conversation. I haven’t fished for compliments since I was thirteen! 

Why were you, of all people, chosen to teach me humility? To make me want to be special and singular to you, to be so possessive over that position that I have to choose not to think about certain things lest I fixate on them and feel ill? 

If you asked me why do you want children, I’d say matter-of-factly that I think there should be more of you in this world, that I want to experience all of life and being human, and that you’d be the best person to share such an extreme comedic tragic tumultuous experience with. How could some abstract mewling infant compare with what i feel for you?

Go ‘way and race your cars.

Completely yours, under protest,
Divya
