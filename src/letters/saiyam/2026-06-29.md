Dear Saiyam,

We started our day at a labyrinthine fruit market, breakfasting on cut fruit and tacos and coffee.  I regret to inform you we spent a good chunk of our afternoon at a local mall, at the H&M, but I had a good time exploring while **mis amigos** shopped. I bought two pairs of heels from a local Columbian brand for dirt cheap, and they're actually beautiful and comfortable.

Many people assume the comfort comes from a large heel, but that’s actually not true. It’s about the placement of the heel and how it helps your weight distribute across the foot. 

A lovely small stiletto right in the middle of your heel is more ergonomic than a solid block too far back. I learned that the Spanish word for socks is **calcetines**. In Mexico they abbreviate it to **calcetas**, but in Columbia there's an apparently totally distinct colloquialism, **medias**. 

I also learned there is a torrenting website called “seedr” which is basically a caching layer on top of Pirate Bay. If someone has torrented your file recently, they just send you a download link directly. 

UberEats does not work in Columbia - I wonder why. Instead there’s an app called Rappio that does food delivery in Latam. Apparently Abhi knows the founders from years ago in YC and was pleased to see their company was still around

The people of this country are very warm and gracious. We were worried about getting pickpocketed, but I dropped cash and left behind my phone at least 3 times today, and each time someone called after me to return it. As I write this I’m chuckling, imagining your expression reading that last sentence. 

Zindagi Na Milegi Dobara led to many interesting side conversations. How to forgive someone you loved who hurt or betrayed you, for example. Personally for me I focus my emotions on what is good and lovable about people while my rational faculties unemotionally evaluate their flaws. When someone hurts me, I see it as weakness, pettiness, or blindness, not malice (god knows I’m free of any of those, so who am I to judge?) and simply adjust my estimation of them accordingly. Dhruv found this level of compartmentalization highly admirable but a little psychopathic.

When we were having dinner at an (amazing!) rooftop restaurant, Dhruv talked about how a ten day totally silent vipassana meditation retreat helped him recover from deep depression he fell into in college.

When questioned about my demons, I thought aloud about not feeling like I've built anything substantial or solid in my life, how that fills me with a certain background anxiety that I am neglecting the bottom of Maslow’s hierarchy in favor of cultivating the top.  

Abhi said he understood why I felt that way and that nothing he said would change it probably, but in his opinion exceptional people should not worry about this, because they will always find a way to create value and keep themselves afloat honestly. Dhruv chimed in that he truly believed if I lost everything it would be only a matter of time before I found my way back. I said perhaps I just bamboozled them by being very good at talking. They laughed and they weren't so easily fooled. 

Predictably, it didn't reduce my anxiety, but it did make me feel warm and fuzzy inside, mostly because I think Dhruv and Abhi do deal with a lot of people who are trying to project more competance than they have. 

They then gave me some very good advice on expressing my opinions in a way that is calibrated with my actual confidence level. "The most cracked researcher at Anthorpic expresses all his opinions as questions."

Truly I'm not trying to come off like I'm certain about everything I say, but I think I talk the way I write, maximally punchy. Caveats make for bad writing but more trustworthy speech. As I mulled over what they said I realized you do this really well.

In the movie, Kabir held Natasha in his arms and nuzzled her head, and for a moment I could feel your lips on my hair. In Memoirs of a Geisha, Sayuri describes confessing her love for the Chairman, after longing for him in silence for many many years, how he gathered her into his arms “just as if she were a blanket” and kissed the hollow of her throat. 

As I write this I’m imagining you caught in the rain, one lucky drop falling into your collar, trickling down your skin, being warmed by your broad and fragrant chest. I feel practically desolate by comparison.

Yours, Divya
