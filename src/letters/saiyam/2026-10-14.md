Dear Saiyam,

I have been reading the stories of Rabindranath Tagore. There was a very charming miniseries on Netflix that did one of his stories per episode. It had none of the drama or maximalism of typical indian productions. The cinematography perfectly captures the rainy, austere beauty of colonial Bengal. [This song, in particular,](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gS6YjQdrIN8&list=RDgS6YjQdrIN8&start_radio=1) composed by Tagore himself, has been stuck in my head. That video has scenes from the show + a translation of the songs’s lyrics. I think it’s absolutely haunting.

Tagore’s stories remind me of Jane Austen’s. They are occupied with the many small absurdities of the human ego, the everyday tragedy of difficult relationships, and wry humor.

I can’t believe you came down to the formula shop to be on hand just in case I needed help with moving. I pulled it off pretty well, didn’t I? Who said I’m bad at planning?

I realized I’m not bad at planning, I just don’t value (most of the time) the smoothness it lends life. I sort of like having to deal with unexpected troubles, the sudden adrenaline and scrambling around and improvisation they require, the eventual, exhausted victory. But as you pointed out, without planning it’s very hard to fit everything you want into one life. What fluency is to coding interviews, what organization is to a living space, planning is to time! Well, when one puts it like that….

What was it about that day? What was it about that autumn air, bright with sun, crisp with impending frost? How did it pull us both back so powerfully to the people we used to be? Like the days of auld lang syne was just yesterday? You’re not given to nostalgia, but even you were a student again. And I was so laden with grief I could hardly breathe.

Oh, my love. I know it hardly matters in the long scheme of life, but I’m glad I’ve known you from (effectively) the very beginning. My time in Toronto was precious and pivotal and I’m glad you were there. I’m glad I was there for at least part of your college years. When you say you love me in all forms I know it’s true.

This week has been a blur of walks to Knott park, chai in clay cups, a parade of Patel aunties. Your parents and sister are very very nice. I cannot help but notice parallels in your parents’ dynamic and ours. I think your mother and I will be very good friends but I hope we never end up at loggerheads. Your “flexible” weasel can be immensely stubborn and disagreeable when she wants to be.

![Saiyam with his parents and sister](/images/letter-2026-10-14-family.jpg)

When they asked me what I liked about you, I felt very shy and didn’t really know what to say. Prosaically, your head is up your ass less than anyone I’ve ever met. And when your mother asked me what I didn’t like about you…I didn’t really have an answer. He could be better at drawing people out in conversation? He lacks color sense? He doesn’t make fun of me as much as he used to? He squirts Tapatio on my incomparable pasta sauces?

The thing is, it’s very suss! My honest-to-god biggest concern about our future is that I should be able to come up with real things I don’t like about you! If I hadn’t known you for four years, I’d be actively trying to figure out what I’m not seeing. If I were to assemble a man like a Build-A-Bear, I can think of things I’d pick differently. But gosh, I’ve never wanted to change a thing about you, from your flannels to your crooked smile, because you wouldn’t be *you*.

I will always, always believe people’s living spaces become a reflection of who they are, no matter how much or little effort they put into them. And even when you were living with cam and mingshi, with your twin bed and folding table desk, I always felt drawn to yours. Everything cheap and functional and little mismatched and little ugly but so, so beautiful, as a whole, in its cleanness and orderliness and feeling of peace. I remember peering in with curiosity the few times I came to the apartment at 89 McGill, and having the mad intrusive thought of wondering what it would be like to lie on your bed…

Well, Divya of 3 years ago, I can confirm it is very nice. Is it funny to say that I miss Saiyam of 3 years ago, my eternally lighthearted, detached, sporadic friend, totally focused on his work and revealing nothing, really, about himself, as elusive as a summer breeze?

![Playing pool](/images/letter-2026-10-14-pool-hall.jpg)

I am amazed and grateful for all the care and trust and intimate knowledge of yourself (and your loved ones) you’ve given me. I know how significant and rare it is. I want nothing more than to continue merging our lives into a divine whole.

But I also wish the people we once were could meet, one more time, at the door to my old apartment. A girl and a boy jaunting off to Honor Snack bar with nothing heavier between them than a shared love of dark jokes.

![Saiyam drinking a Fanta](/images/letter-2026-10-14-tapatio.jpg)

The boy who’d watch me drink with drug addicts with only an incidental worry for my wellbeing. The girl who listened to all your painful and risky endeavors with a carefree heart, and watched you walk away from her with no desire to run after you. We were both wanderers in our own way.

Oh, Saiyam, we had the best time, didn’t we?

Your girl,
Divya
