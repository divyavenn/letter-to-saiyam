Dear Saiyam,

Yesterday we spent the night at my house (for the first time in a month) and got bit by mosquitos. The sheets smelled too strongly of detergent, and my hair was a total mess the day after.

I didn’t sleep well, and I don’t think you did either, but there was something curiously nice about drifting in and out of sleep throughout the night. All sorts of strange images and desires and words started to meld in my brain. Like I was water, and you were a stone, sinking but not fast enough; like the bed was covered in a thin, glittering layer of frost; like I was grass and you hay; and I was only meant to patiently endure your weight, like you were alive and I was dead, and I was straining towards you, a lonely nightghast that wanted to bet alive and warm and loved.

That last bit was probably quite literal. I feel like I chased you all the way across the bed at least twice.

I should get another one of our favorite blanket for my place.

I am behind on work, and I still have to write and film today’s video, but I will try get it all done before I see you. I want spend tonight relaxing with you, but not in front of the TV. Reading on your bed, maybe. Or listening to Rudyard Kipling’s short stories while walking through the Presidio.

Watch [Nepthali](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7peyXHKX9dw&list=RD7peyXHKX9dw&start_radio=1) and [Duet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x0Y35XLBY8A&list=RDx0Y35XLBY8A&start_radio=1), two lovely short animations by Glen Keane. This is how I want to feel tonight, light as air. You may be interesting in the [making of Nepthali](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wsQ4--X2ls4). You can see Glen Keane had quite the fascination with ballerinas. I always wish I studied ballet. It’s remarkable how much discipline it takes to achieve any measure of freedom. How strong your muscles have to be to move with the easy grace of a whirlwind, how focused and clean your mind must be to not get ensnared by endless distractions.

I have been thinking very wistfully about our future home together. And about libraries! A sunny, warm, very quiet room, the floor covered with tatami mats, bookshelves covering every wall, a place to stretch and pick a book at random. No outlets. A small japanese maple. A vase with one flower. A candle and some matches. The hot scent of pages and straw and wax and cotton, with a thin thread of roses.

I can’t wait to build it with be there with you, but I have often found anticipating a thing is just as fun as getting it, haven’t you?

Love,
Divya
